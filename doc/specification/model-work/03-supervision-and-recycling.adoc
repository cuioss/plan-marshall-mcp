[#supervisor]
== Supervisor
_See Requirements link:../../Requirements.adoc#PM-WORK-3[PM-WORK-3: Supervision & Recycling], link:../../Requirements.adoc#PM-WF-14[PM-WF-14: Task Envelopes, Model Slots & Usage per Task]_

Neither the transport nor the model reports that a worker is lost or about to give up. The runtime runs one supervisor per project and role. It keeps a worker available for every queued task of the role (xref:02-task-protocol.adoc#queue-task[Queue Task]), detects lost workers, ends and replaces them, and recycles warm workers before they give up or their context grows too large. A worker is a job of kind `llm` (link:../job-runtime.adoc[Job Runtime Specification]); its job record carries its role, form, `generation_id`, and worker state (xref:02-task-protocol.adoc#worker-states[Worker States]; link:../store-schemas/10-job-and-lesson-records.adoc#job-record[Store Schemas Specification, Job Record]). The launch of every worker is the launch contract of xref:04-worker-launch.adoc[Worker Launch].

* *Fresh roles*: For every queued task of the role, the supervisor starts one fresh worker for that task, up to the role's `count` of running fresh workers in the project. The worker ends after its task closes.
* *Warm roles*: While the role has queued tasks, the supervisor keeps up to the role's `count` warm workers of the project, each started for one working directory (a plan's worktree or the project checkout) and serving only the tasks that run there, because a worker's working directory is fixed at launch (xref:04-worker-launch.adoc#job-working-directory[Worker Launch, Working Directory]). Without queued work it starts none; idle workers end by the idle recycle (<<recycling>>).
* *Bounds*: Every start also needs a model slot and respects one writer per plan (<<warm-slots>>).

[#host-values]
=== Values per Host and per Role

The supervisor takes its timing from the client profile of the worker's harness (link:../client-profiles/01-profile-schema.adoc[Client Profiles Specification, Worker Block]) and its recycling rules from the role's artifact (xref:01-roles-and-configuration.adoc#role-artifact[Role Artifact Schema]). No value is compiled per role or per harness into the supervisor.

|===
|Value |Source |Use

|Bounded wait, progress frames
|`connection.bounded_wait_seconds`, `connection.progress_resets_timeout`
|How long a `pm_wait` is held, and whether progress frames are sent (xref:02-task-protocol.adoc#pm-wait[`pm_wait`]).

|Acknowledgement deadline, informational
|`worker.ack_deadline_seconds`, `worker.ack_informational`
|<<detection>>

|Silence grace

|===

[#detection]
== Detection
_See Requirement link:../../Requirements.adoc#PM-WORK-3[PM-WORK-3: Supervision & Recycling]_

The supervisor ends a worker as `lost` on any of these signals, and on no other:

* *Exit*: The worker's process exits without the server's instruction after its first call. An exit before the first call is a start failure (<<start-failure-guard>>).
* *Missing acknowledgement*: An offer is not acknowledged within the host's acknowledgement deadline (xref:02-task-protocol.adoc#ack[Acknowledge]), where the host's acknowledgement is not informational.
* *Silence*: The worker has no open call (a held `pm_wait` or `pm_build` is an open call) and has made no other call for longer than the bounded wait plus the host's silence grace. For a worker that has not made its first call, the time counts from its spawn, and its end counts as a start failure (<<start-failure-guard>>).

A `zero_tool` worker (the screener) has no MCP connection and makes no call, so none of these signals applies to it: it receives no offer to acknowledge, it is never silent, and it is never counted by the start-failure guard (<<start-failure-guard>>). Its fate is decided by its exit and its worker budget alone (link:../timeouts/01-job-budgets-and-waits.adoc#model-job-budget[Timeouts & Budgets Specification, Worker Budget]): an exit with a valid result closes its task `submitted`, every other exit and the expiry of its budget close it `lost` (xref:07-untrusted-text-screening.adoc#screener[Screener]).

Liveness never rests on the transport: a held call the host abandoned does not keep a worker alive past its silence limit, and no signal ends a worker earlier than its deadline. A lost worker's lease is released at its fence (<<ending>>), and its task is offered again (xref:02-task-protocol.adoc#re-offer[Re-Offer]).

[#recycling]
== Recycling Warm Workers
_See Requirements link:../../Requirements.adoc#PM-WORK-3[PM-WORK-3: Supervision & Recycling], link:../../Requirements.adoc#PM-WF-14[PM-WF-14: Task Envelopes, Model Slots & Usage per Task]_

_Implementation watch: link:../../implementation-watch/model-work.adoc#PM-WATCH-DISP-43[PM-WATCH-DISP-43]_

A warm worker is recycled by the answer `end` to its next `pm_wait` (<<ending>>), never in the middle of a task. The supervisor recycles it when:

* *Idle*: it received its idle wake-ups in a row as `wait_again` answers without a task. A successor is started only when a task of the role is queued again.
* *Token budget*: its context reaches the role's `token_budget`. The context of a worker is the input, cache-read, and cache-creation tokens of its latest turn, taken from the harness's per-turn usage through the profile's usage pointers (the same per-turn usage that is accounted per task, xref:04-worker-launch.adoc[Worker Launch]); a run-total record is never read as a context size. The budget recycles a generation only after it closed at least one task: the context a worker starts with differs by harness and model and can exceed a small budget, which would otherwise recycle every generation before its first task.
* *Tasks per generation*: it closed the role's `tasks_per_generation` tasks.
* *Changed artifact*: the role's configuration digest (xref:01-roles-and-configuration.adoc#role-artifact[Role Artifact Schema]) differs from the one the generation was started with (xref:01-roles-and-configuration.adoc#role-sets[Role Sets & Precedence]).

A successor for queued work is started at once after the recycle. Recycling needs no handover: the successor starts with an empty context and receives every skill again (xref:02-task-protocol.adoc#leases-fencing[Leases, Generations & Fencing]).

[#ending]
== Ending a Worker
_See Requirement link:../../Requirements.adoc#PM-WORK-3[PM-WORK-3: Supervision & Recycling]_

* *Instructed end*: The server answers the worker's `pm_wait` with `end`: after a fresh worker's task closed, on a recycle, and on a drain. A worker that does not exit within the termination grace is ended as below.
* *Forced end*: A lost worker, and a worker that did not exit after `end`, is ended by `SIGTERM`, then `SIGKILL` after the termination grace, by the termination protocol of every job (link:../job-runtime/01-pipeline-and-subprocesses.adoc#_process_termination_cancellation_protocol[Job Runtime Specification, Process Termination & Cancellation Protocol]).
* *Fence*: Then its job token is revoked, its generation fenced, and its lease released at once (xref:02-task-protocol.adoc#fencing[Fencing]). A successor is started when the role still has queued work.
* *Fate and usage*: The job's fate is derived by the job runtime from what it observed (link:../job-runtime/01-pipeline-and-subprocesses.adoc#outcome-derivation[Job Runtime Specification, Outcome Derivation]). A worker ended by a signal reports no run usage; its run usage is recorded as `unmeasured`, while the per-turn usage of the turns it completed stays accounted to their tasks.

On a drain (link:../runtime-model.adoc[Runtime & Interaction Model Specification]) the runtime offers no new task, answers the waits of idle warm workers with `end`, lets running tasks continue up to the drain timeout, and then ends the remaining workers by force; their leases are released and their tasks offered again after the next start, without counting a re-offer.

[#start-failure-guard]
== Start-Failure Guard
_See Requirement link:../../Requirements.adoc#PM-WORK-3[PM-WORK-3: Supervision & Recycling]_

A worker of a `writer` or `read_only` role that exits before its first call (its job record has no `connected_at`) has failed to start; it is not lost. A `zero_tool` worker makes no call and is never counted here (<<detection>>). A harness whose account is at its usage limit, or that cannot start at all, makes every worker exit this way, and replacing it would start workers without end. The guard:

. *Count*: The supervisor counts consecutive start failures per role and harness; the first call of any worker of the role on that harness resets the count. A launch refused before spawn counts as a start failure as well (xref:04-worker-launch.adoc#harness-selection[Worker Launch, Launch Refusals]). A fresh worker's task is queued again without counting a re-offer.
. *Hold*: After the compiled number of consecutive start failures (link:../timeouts/01-job-budgets-and-waits.adoc#start-failure-guard[Timeouts & Budgets Specification, Start-Failure Guard]), the guard holds: the supervisor starts no worker of the role on that harness. The queued tasks of the role and every new one close `lost` with the reason `role_unavailable` and take their conservative fallback (xref:01-roles-and-configuration.adoc#conservative-fallback[Conservative Fallback]).
. *Report*: The harness's own error text (the last lines of the failed worker's output, bounded to 2 KiB and redacted like every job log) is reported to the operator in `pm-operator status`, in the web app with a notification of the paired devices, and in the audit log, with the role and the harness.
. *Clear*: The guard clears when the role's artifact changes or the harness is enrolled again. After the compiled probe backoff (link:../timeouts/01-job-budgets-and-waits.adoc#start-failure-guard[Timeouts & Budgets Specification, Start-Failure Guard]), the next task of the role starts one worker as a probe and waits for it instead of closing: a probe that makes its first call clears the guard; a probe that fails closes the task `role_unavailable` and restarts the backoff.

[#start-collisions]
=== Starts of One Harness

Concurrent starts of one harness can collide in the harness's local state (OpenCode's local database is locked by a second process that starts at the same moment). The supervisors therefore start the workers of one harness one after another on the machine: the next start waits until the previous worker made its first call or ended, at most the compiled spacing bound (link:../timeouts/01-job-budgets-and-waits.adoc#harness-start-spacing[Timeouts & Budgets Specification, Start Spacing of One Harness]). A collision that still makes a worker exit before its first call is a start failure and counts for the guard.

[#warm-slots]
== Model Slots & Warm Workers
_See Requirement link:../../Requirements.adoc#PM-WF-14[PM-WF-14: Task Envelopes, Model Slots & Usage per Task]_

Every worker holds a machine-wide model slot from its spawn to its exit, a warm worker for its whole generation, idle or not; the slot rules and one writer per plan are specified in xref:04-worker-launch.adoc#model-slots[Worker Launch, Model Slots & One Writer per Plan]. The supervisor adds two rules:

* *Idle recycle returns slots*: The idle recycle (<<recycling>>) is what returns the slot of an unused warm worker.
* *Idle yields*: When a task of another role waits for a slot and every slot is held, the supervisor recycles the longest-idle warm worker that holds no lease, so idle warm workers never starve queued work.
