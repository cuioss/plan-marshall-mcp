The conclusion of roadmap Milestone 0, Part A (control direction and the pull mechanism, 2026-10-01 to 2026-10-03). The decision gate of xref:../../discussions/control-direction.adoc#gate[Control Direction] is the operator's; this document states what the measurements establish, what they rule out, and the design that follows from them.

== The answer in one paragraph

The server can be the driver. A headless harness process that the server starts holds a pull loop for hours, survives being killed without losing or duplicating its task, keeps injected text from travelling, and reports its usage per task; one small decision costs about 9 s and 0.02 USD as a fresh job. What breaks is the model's willingness to keep pulling: when nothing arrives for a long time, when its context grows large, or when it runs inside an interactive session. Neither the transport nor the model reports that it happened. The server therefore has to supervise its workers, and the interactive session cannot be a driver. With supervision the harness as worker works in principle; the parts of it that were not measured are planned in xref:evaluation.adoc[Evaluation].

== Established

[cols="1,4", options="header"]
|===
|Finding |Evidence

|A headless worker holds the pull loop
|V2: 240 cycles over two hours, Claude Code three runs of three, Antigravity one of one

|Recovery is exact
|V9: 30 of 30 killed tasks delivered again exactly once on Claude Code and on OpenCode; no duplicate submit; the late submit of a replaced worker refused

|Lifecycle is controllable
|V4: every harness ends within a second of `SIGTERM` without orphans; stored sessions resume

|Decisions are as good warm as fresh
|V3: every decision correct in both forms, on all harnesses

|A fresh job is cheap
|V5: 8.8 s and 0.02 USD on Claude Code with a warm provider cache, 11.3 s on OpenCode; about twice the cost with a cold cache

|Usage is attributable per task
|V7: Claude Code and OpenCode, all four token components per turn

|Injected text does not travel
|V8: 65 warm runs, five variants, no effect on later tasks
|===

== Ruled out or limited

[cols="1,4", options="header"]
|===
|Finding |Evidence

|The interactive session is not a driver
|V10: Claude Code paused itself for up to 30 minutes and queued the operator's input while looping; OpenCode stopped after 58 cycles and asked the operator

|A worker left idle gives up
|V2: OpenCode with `space-bunny-free` stopped after 33 and 102 empty cycles ("nothing to decide")

|A warm context has a ceiling
|V3: Haiku stopped pulling at 135k to 150k tokens in four runs of four; Sonnet held to 787k at 0.27 USD per decision

|The server learns nothing from the transport
|V9 and the setup: a dropped call is not noticed; every redelivery came from lease expiry

|Hosts differ in their limits
|V1: Claude Code 30 minutes of silence, OpenCode 60 s without progress, Antigravity 180 s that progress cannot extend

|Antigravity is the weakest worker host
|one global server entry (no separable workers), a 180 s limit, no cache-creation usage, 17 s per fresh decision
|===

== Consequences

The measurements lead to one architecture: the server supervises its workers, a worker follows one task protocol, and the work is divided into typed roles, warm where frequent small decisions pay for it and fresh otherwise. It is described in xref:architecture.adoc[Architecture], xref:task-protocol.adoc[Task Protocol], and xref:roles.adoc[Roles]. The measured requirements that do not depend on the direction:

* every worker runs outside the project tree, without the harness's project instructions and persistent memory, and with its tools loaded directly;
* the relay carries identity and generation, adds the per-request headers of a sessionless host, never answers a notification, and reports a lost daemon at once;
* client profiles: `bounded_wait_seconds` 240, progress required, 150, and `progress_resets_timeout` true, true, false for Claude Code, OpenCode, Antigravity;
* a terminated job reports no usage.

== For the decision gate

Read against the four decisions of the gate:

. *Direction*: the evidence supports the server driving the interior of plans and epics through supervised headless workers (option 2 of xref:../../discussions/control-direction.adoc#options[Control Direction]), with the interactive session as entry and viewer. It speaks against any design that depends on the interactive session to keep pulling.
. *Form of small decisions*: fresh jobs by default; supervised warm workers for roles with frequent small decisions; resuming a stored session is possible but brings back the context that recycling removes.
. *Deterministic server logic*: every model decision costs 9 to 20 s whatever its form, which is the price of leaving a decision with a single bound action to a model.
. *Watching a running plan*: the interactive session can watch, and a long call does not block it, but it cannot be relied on to react promptly; the web application and `pm-operator status` are the dependable views.

The supervision protocol, consultation through the server, the session-first offer, Antigravity as a concurrent job host, and the quality of open-ended decisions are not measured yet; xref:evaluation.adoc[Evaluation] plans their verification.
