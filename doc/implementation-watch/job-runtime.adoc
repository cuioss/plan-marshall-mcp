[#PM-WATCH-JOB-3]
=== PM-WATCH-JOB-3: A git timeout is not a refusal

Anchor:: link:../specification/job-runtime.adoc#_process_termination_cancellation_protocol[Job Runtime § Process Termination & Cancellation Protocol],
link:../specification/job-runtime.adoc#in-process-operations[Job Runtime § In-Process Operations],

fields.

[#PM-WATCH-JOB-5]
=== PM-WATCH-JOB-5: Bounded waits are wall-clock and say what happened

Anchor:: link:../specification/job-runtime.adoc#_bounded_long_poll_progress_reset_waiting_primitives_pm_wf_8_pm_impl_4[Job Runtime § Bounded Long-Poll & Progress-Reset Waiting Primitives],
link:../specification/timeouts.adoc#waits[Timeouts & Budgets § Waits & Long-Polls],
link:../Requirements.adoc#PM-IMPL-4[PM-IMPL-4]
Hazard:: A wait budget passed as an iteration count expired in seconds and reported a bot "absent" while it was
running; a wait at its ceiling could not tell waiting from re-polling finished work; wait cost was invisible.
Guard:: Wait jobs use monotonic wall-clock deadlines (test with fast pollers); an exhausted poll is `unproven`, not
absent; a bounded wait returning `running` reports whether the job state changed during the window; a wait on an
already-settled job returns immediately; time deferred by a provider rate limit is attributed `provider_rate_limit`,
never counted as a timeout. The wait population below is the baseline for bounded-wait metrics.
Fixture:: 600 s budget burned in seconds ("CodeRabbit absent" while running); a script cost rollup of 2,051 calls, 14,423,580 ms, dominated by the three wait notations
build_server 27.4%, ci 27.3%, ci_complete_precondition 23.1% (77.8% together). Wait attribution by reason:
deliberate (an operator ruling to wait out a CodeRabbit quota window) vs poll miss (8 `ci_complete_precondition`
polls averaging 417 s), never one "waste" figure.
Source:: review-apparatus PLAN-PR-048 D2; lessons-routing lesson 2026-09-23-05-003 (`.plan/orchestrator/orchestrator-refactor/inbox/archive/verdict-staleness-scoping/verdict-staleness-scoping-003.md`); post-run-quality PLAN-PRQ-11 D0
and D2.

[#PM-WATCH-JOB-19]
=== PM-WATCH-JOB-19: One shared provider poll honours `304` and `Retry-After`

Anchor:: link:../specification/job-runtime.adoc#_bounded_long_poll_progress_reset_waiting_primitives_pm_wf_8_pm_impl_4[Job Runtime § Bounded Long-Poll & Progress-Reset Waiting Primitives],
link:../specification/timeouts.adoc#ci-repoll-cadence[Timeouts & Budgets § Provider Re-Poll Cadence]
Hazard:: Every wait polls the provider on its own and ignores rate-limit signals; the credential's rate budget is
exhausted, and the throttling is reported as a timeout or as a failure of the checks being waited for.
Guard:: Recorded provider with `ETag` support and a rate limit: two concurrent waits on the same `(credential,
resource)` cause one request per 30 s cadence; a `304` is an observation that changes nothing; a rate-limit answer
with `Retry-After` or a reset instant defers the next request to that instant, the wait slices carry the reason
`provider_rate_limit`, the deferral is not counted against the wait's budget, and the wait never ends `timed_out`
because of it.
Source:: PM-MCP git and provider integration design review, operator decisions of 2026-09-28 (Z2-27); no plan-marshall defect record.

== Linux Filesystem Confinement (Landlock)

[#PM-WATCH-JOB-20]
=== PM-WATCH-JOB-20: A job cannot read the runtime token

Anchor:: link:../specification/job-runtime.adoc#_linux_filesystem_confinement_landlock[Job Runtime § Linux Filesystem Confinement] (Read set),
link:../Requirements.adoc#PM-SEC-5[PM-SEC-5]
Hazard:: Landlock rules only grant access, so a read rule on `/` also grants `<PM_MCP_BASE>/run/`; a job that reads
`runtime.token` can call every endpoint of the local API, the operator endpoints included.
Guard:: On Linux with Landlock, a job fixture tries to read `<PM_MCP_BASE>/run/runtime.token` and fails with a
permission error, while it reads a sibling entry of `run/` under `<PM_MCP_BASE>` and a sibling of each directory on
the path from `/` (read set built by sibling enumeration); control: an entry created on that path after job start is
not readable by the job, which is the accepted limitation, and an entry created elsewhere stays readable.

== Adaptive Build Timeouts, Coverage Parsers & Gate Honesty

[#PM-WATCH-JOB-15]
=== PM-WATCH-JOB-15: A timeout over a passing build is no verdict

Anchor:: link:../specification/job-runtime.adoc#_adaptive_timeout_learning[Job Runtime § Adaptive Timeout Learning],
link:../specification/timeouts.adoc#job-budgets[Timeouts & Budgets § Job Budgets & Escalation],
link:../Requirements.adoc#PM-EXEC-6[PM-EXEC-6]
Hazard:: Timeouts were read as failures.
Guard:: Matched tests: a failing build stays `failed`; a timeout over a build that would pass yields no verdict; an
unlearned budget is recorded as `budget_derivation: floor_default` in the job record (never rendered) and its timeout
offers `retry` with the escalated budget instead of failing the gate.
Fixture:: job `dbbc60f9` (an integration test that normally takes 1250–1800 s timed out at 300 s before any test ran and was resubmitted as `abe890bd`);
plugin-doctor real-tree test over 300 s under `module-tests`, green under `verify` (one test red in one runner and
green in another, invisibly); a 30 s budget against a verb measured at 24–57 s.
Source:: truthful-signals PLAN-TRUTH-169 D3, D4.

[#PM-WATCH-JOB-16]
=== PM-WATCH-JOB-16: A timed-out or residue-poisoned run never inflates the learned budget
